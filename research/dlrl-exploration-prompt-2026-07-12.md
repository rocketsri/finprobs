# Task: DL/RL Extensions to the log S-fBM Volatility Research Program

## Background

Deep Hedging (Buehler, Gonon, Teichmann & Wood, 2019, arXiv:1802.03042) established that a neural network hedging policy can be trained end-to-end, directly from simulated price paths, to minimize a convex risk measure (CVaR or entropic risk) of terminal hedging P&L under realistic frictions (transaction costs, discrete rebalancing) — without ever needing a closed-form pricing PDE, Greeks, or an analytic model. This matters here specifically because it decouples "does this model have practical value" from "can we build a closed-form pricing engine for it" — the latter was flagged in this project's own Direction 4 report as a large, uncertain undertaking (`research/log-sfbm-unification-2026-07-12.md`, "what would most efficiently move this forward," item 3) that has not been attempted. Deep hedging is a way to test the same practical-payoff question via simulation and learning instead.

**Established facts (verify in Step 0 per track, not re-derived here):**
- Horvath, Muguruza & Tomas (2021, arXiv:1901.09647) apply deep learning to rough volatility, but as a *calibration surrogate* (a network approximating the model→implied-vol-surface map for fast calibration), not as a hedging policy. Do not conflate this with deep hedging when checking whether "DL + rough vol" has already been done.
- Multi-agent market simulation with learned (RL) trading agents is an active area (Ganesh et al., 2019, arXiv:1911.05892, "Reinforcement Learning for Market Making"; Vyetrenko et al., 2019/2020, arXiv:1911.11455, "Get Real: Realism Metrics for Market Simulation"; the ABIDES simulator, Byrd et al., 2019, arXiv:1904.12066). These focus on realism metrics like fat tails, volatility clustering, and order-book statistics. Whether any of this literature specifically tests for genuine multifractal/cascade structure (as opposed to fat tails and clustering, which are much weaker and more easily reproduced properties) is unverified — check in Step 0.

**This project's own already-established groundwork (reusable — read and reuse, do not re-derive):**
- `research/log-sfbm-unification-2026-07-12.md` and `research/papers/paper0-synthesis.tex` — full context, the model's actual definition (§"Ground truth"), and the central finding that the roughness (H) dimension is well-supported while the multifractal/intermittency (λ²) dimension is the weakest-supported part of the whole framework.
- `research/papers/paper4-spx-vix-calibration.tex` — Theorem 1 (proved and numerically verified): at fixed H, λ² cancels out exactly of the ratio between short-maturity and VIX-horizon log-volatility covariance; T is the parameter that actually controls it. This was verified only at the level of the covariance formula itself — never against an actual hedging or pricing use case.
- `research/papers/paper3-microstructural-derivation.tex` — three candidate microstructural mechanisms for producing genuine cascade structure (plain Hawkes superposition, quadratic/Zumbach Hawkes, exact criticality) were each mathematically **ruled out**, narrowing the open problem to one unproven double-scaling limit theorem. This was pursued purely analytically; no computational/learned alternative route has been tried.
- `research/scripts/aggregation_simulation.py` and `research/scripts/test_lambda_cancellation.py` — working Cholesky-based simulators for log S-fBM-type Gaussian log-vol paths (both the power-law and log-covariance regimes) and the closed-form covariance-ratio checker. Reuse this simulation machinery directly rather than re-deriving path generation.
- `research/scripts/shuffle_surrogate_test.py` — a genuineness diagnostic (distributional vs. temporal multiscaling), already validated by Monte Carlo against known ground truth and already applied to real market data in this project. Reuse this exact diagnostic to test any new simulated or emergent price series produced below.

## The gap

Two gaps combine into a DL/RL opportunity this research program has not touched, because everything so far has been pursued by closed-form derivation or classical estimation, never by learning or simulation-as-a-first-class-method:

**(a) Practical payoff, untested by hedging.** Theorem 1 (λ² cancels, T is the lever) was verified only against the model's covariance formula directly. Whether this translates into an actual hedging advantage — i.e., whether a hedging policy that has access to the richer (H, λ², T) structure achieves lower realized hedging cost/risk on a portfolio spanning both a short-dated option and a VIX-like instrument, compared to a policy restricted to plain rough volatility — has never been tested. Deep hedging is the natural way to test this without building a full pricing engine.

**(b) Genuine cascade structure, untested by learning.** All three ruled-out microstructural mechanisms in Direction 3 were hand-specified (a fixed Hawkes kernel, a fixed quadratic feedback term, a fixed critical branching ratio). None of them let agent behavior *adapt*. A multi-agent RL market simulation, where trading policies are learned rather than hand-derived, is a fundamentally different route to the same open question: if learned multi-timescale trading behavior produces genuine multiplicative cascade structure (testable directly with this project's own validated shuffle-surrogate diagnostic), that is informative even without a closed-form limit theorem. If it does not, that is itself a meaningful negative result reinforcing how structurally hard the analytic gap is — consistent with, not contradicting, the existing negative results.

## Track A — Deep hedging under log S-fBM: does the model add practical value?

**Step 0.** Search specifically for: deep hedging applied to rough volatility models (any H<1/2 driver), deep hedging combined with a multifractal/intermittency parameter, and any deep-hedging paper that jointly hedges an index option and a variance/VIX-like instrument in one portfolio (the joint-instrument setup is the part most likely to be genuinely absent — confirm or refute). If an exact match already exists, say so and stop; if a close relative exists, note precisely how this differs.

**Step 1.** Extend `research/scripts/aggregation_simulation.py`'s Cholesky-based log S-fBM simulator to a full price-process simulator: $S_t = S_0 \exp\!\big(\int_0^t (\rho\, dW_s^\omega \sqrt{2} - \tfrac12 e^{\omega_s})\,ds + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\int_0^t e^{\omega_s/2} dW_s^\perp\big)$-style construction (a leverage-correlated price process driven by the log S-fBM log-vol $\omega_t$ from Eq.~(1) in `paper4-spx-vix-calibration.tex`) — log S-fBM itself has no leverage/price-dynamics component in the source paper, so this correlation must be added explicitly, the same way rough Bergomi adds it to plain rough volatility; state the assumption plainly rather than presenting it as part of the original model.

**Step 2.** Implement a Buehler-style deep hedging network (small feedforward or LSTM cell per rebalancing step, PyTorch, CPU-scale) trained to minimize CVaR of terminal hedging P&L for a short-dated option on $S_t$, with transaction costs. **Correctness test:** in the limit $H\to1/2$, $\lambda^2\to0$ (reduces to a standard lognormal/Black-Scholes-like process), confirm the learned hedge converges to something close to standard delta hedging and the hedging error matches the known Black-Scholes hedging-error benchmark, before trusting the network on the full rough/multifractal regime.

**Step 3.** Add a second instrument to the hedged portfolio: a variance-swap-like payoff on the same path (a VIX-analog, e.g. realized variance over the next 21 trading days), and train the same network to jointly hedge both. Run the sensitivity experiment implied by Theorem 1: vary $\lambda^2$ at fixed $H,T$ and check whether realized hedging performance is roughly invariant (matching the theorem's prediction that $\lambda^2$ is calibration-inert for this ratio); vary $T$ at fixed $H,\lambda^2$ and check whether it materially changes hedging performance (matching the theorem's prediction that $T$ is the real lever). Use `test_lambda_cancellation.py`'s exact parameter grid so the hedging result is directly comparable to the covariance-level result already established.

**Step 4.** Benchmark the learned hedge against a hand-specified baseline restricted to plain rough volatility (no separate $\lambda^2$/$T$ structure, i.e. the $H\to0$-agnostic rough Bergomi-style special case) at matched effective parameter count, following the same parameter-matched-comparison discipline as the Abi Jaber–Illand–Li precedent already cited in this project's Direction 4 report — the question is whether the richer model structure earns its keep once parameter count is controlled for, not merely whether it fits better with more free parameters.

**Step 5 (conditional).** If Step 3 shows the aggregation-driven $T$-lengthening tension from `paper4-spx-vix-calibration.tex` (Finding, "does the $T$-lever survive aggregation?") actually matters in practice, simulate an aggregated/"index-like" version of the hedged instrument (using `aggregation_simulation.py`'s factor structure) and check whether the hedging-cost picture changes as predicted.

## Track B — Multi-agent RL market microstructure: does emergent cascade structure appear?

**Step 0.** Search specifically for any paper testing multi-agent (rule-based or RL-trained) market simulations for genuine multifractal/cascade volatility structure — not just fat tails or volatility clustering, which are much weaker properties already known to be reproducible by many simple mechanisms (per this project's own Direction 3 findings on quadratic Hawkes and long-range dependence). If this exact test has been run before, report what was found and whether it used a real genuineness diagnostic or just visual/qualitative comparison.

**Step 1.** Build a minimal multi-agent market simulator with at least two timescale populations (e.g., a fast, tightly-coupled market-maker/momentum population and a slower, more diffuse population representing macro-information-driven flow — directly instantiating the speculative economic mechanism proposed in `paper2-aggregation-rg-flow.tex`, Conjecture 1). Implement one hand-specified (rule-based Hawkes-like) baseline version first, matching the already-proven-negative "plain superposition" case from Direction 3 as a sanity check — its resulting volatility series should show only long-range dependence, not genuine multiscaling, confirming the simulator itself is faithful to the established math before trusting it on the RL variant.

**Step 2.** Train simple RL policies (e.g. tabular Q-learning or a small PPO agent — keep this CPU-tractable) for the trading populations with a plausible reward (e.g. inventory-risk-adjusted P&L for market makers), allowing feedback between populations (one population's policy can condition on another's recent realized activity) — this is the "multiplicative coupling" structural ingredient Direction 3 identified as the specific missing piece for genuine cascade behavior, now supplied by learned rather than hand-specified adaptation.

**Step 3.** Apply `research/scripts/shuffle_surrogate_test.py` directly, unmodified in its core logic, to the emergent price/volatility series from both the rule-based baseline and the RL-trained variant. Report the temporal-vs-distributional fraction for each, exactly as computed for the real-market data in this project, so results are directly comparable.

**Step 4.** Compare rule-based baseline vs. RL-trained variant. If the RL variant shows a materially higher genuinely-temporal fraction and a curvature pattern resembling log-covariance rather than power-law (reusing the nested model-selection check from `research/scripts/apply_diagnostic_real_data.py`), report exactly what learned behavior/coupling produced it, as concretely as possible (which population, what state variable it conditions on, what the effective coupling looks like) — this is the part that would actually inform the open double-scaling-limit conjecture in Direction 3, not just confirm or refute it in the abstract.

**Step 5 (conditional).** If a genuine effect is found, check its robustness to random seed and training budget before reporting it as more than a single-run curiosity, given the CPU-only compute constraint stated above.

## Track D — RL optimal execution exploiting the roughness gap (stretch goal)

Single stocks are estimated rougher (more locally clustered volatility bursts) than the index (this project's own real-data finding). An RL execution agent conditioning its trading rate on a real-time local roughness estimate is a natural, literature-adjacent test (Nevmyvaka et al., 2006; Ning, Ling & Jaimungal, 2020, arXiv:1812.06600, "Double Deep Q-Learning for Optimal Execution") of whether that statistical property is exploitable, as opposed to merely descriptive. Treat as a stretch goal only if Tracks A and B are further along.
